This week gives the underlying mechanism a proper name, three weeks after
first meeting it in disguise. **Confirmation bias** is the tendency to notice
and remember evidence that fits a belief you already hold, and to overlook or
explain away evidence that doesn't — it's the reason the hit from a horoscope
sticks and the miss doesn't. **Illusory correlation** is the related tendency
to perceive a statistical relationship between two things that aren't
actually related, particularly when one of them is rare or emotionally
charged — it's why a "lucky" pair of socks survives a decade of losses and
gets credit for the one big win.

Neither bias is a character flaw or a sign of poor reasoning. Both have been
demonstrated repeatedly in controlled experiments, in subjects with no
particular investment in astrology, tarot or omens — they are default
settings of ordinary cognition, not symptoms of gullibility. That is the
important reframe for this stage of the course: everything covered in weeks
3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 can be fully explained without assuming anyone involved is
being irrational. They are being *exactly as rational as human cognition
ordinarily is* — which turns out to be less rational, in specific and
well-mapped ways, than intuition suggests.

This is also the week to look back at your own Pattern Diary with fresh eyes.
Several entries probably describe something that felt meaningfully connected.
Read them again asking: what did I notice, what did I link it to, and would I
have remembered the miss as clearly as I remembered the hit?
