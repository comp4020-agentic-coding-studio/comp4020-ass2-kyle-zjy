Tarot is a **symbol system**: a fixed, shared vocabulary of images — the
Tower, the Hanged Man, the Ten of Swords — rich and ambiguous enough that a
skilled reader (or a determined solo querent) can connect almost any card to
almost any live concern. That flexibility is usually presented as the
tradition's mystical strength. This week treats it as the tradition's most
interesting *mechanical* feature.

Compare tarot to the Rorschach inkblot test: both present ambiguous stimuli
and ask the subject to find meaning in them, and in both cases the meaning
found reveals more about the interpreter's current preoccupations than about
any hidden property of the stimulus. The difference is presentation — a
psychologist frames the inkblot as diagnostic of the reader, a tarot reader
frames the card as diagnostic of the future. Structurally, the interpretive
act is close to identical.

This doesn't make a reading worthless. Externalising a decision onto a card
and being made to argue for an interpretation of it can genuinely surface
things you hadn't said out loud yet — a real psychological function, achieved
through a mechanism that has nothing to do with prophecy. The honest
description is "a structured prompt for reflection," not "a window into what
will happen," and the gap between those two descriptions is most of what this
course is about.

## Try it

Sit with all three readings of the same card before deciding which one you
find most convincing — the "right" answer is closer to the second than the
first, but noticing why the first one is tempting is the actual point.
