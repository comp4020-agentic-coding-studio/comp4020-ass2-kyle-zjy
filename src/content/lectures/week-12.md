Twelve weeks ago, this course opened with a claim: brains built to survive a
noisy, dangerous world keep the same pattern-detecting machinery running in a
world of headlines, horoscopes and recommendation feeds, and that machinery
never got switched off. Everything since has been the same three-part method,
applied to a different case each week — see the pattern, notice how it gets
given meaning, and ask what would actually make it worth trusting.

Week 1 introduced the detector. Weeks 3 through 9 ran it against six
different systems — omens, dreams, Jung, astrology, tarot, I Ching and
numerology — each with its own history and its own vocabulary, and each
running some version of the same interpretive move: notice, link, remember
the hits. Week 10 named the mechanism properly. Week 11 pointed it at
something that predicts you with genuine statistical accuracy rather than
vague plausibility, and asked whether that changes anything.

This week turns the method on the one thing in the course you haven't been
asked to examine yet: the site delivering it. The activity below revisits the
sentence on the homepage — the one that told you exactly when you arrived and
how many times you'd been here before. It was built, deliberately, using the
same techniques studied all semester, disclosed rather than hidden. Naming the
trick doesn't make the sentence stop working. That gap — between explaining a
pattern and still feeling it — is the honest answer to the course's opening
question, and it's what the final project asks you to write about directly.

While you're looking: the small ◇ that has marked every divider on this site
since week 1 — between sections, after the active page in the navigation, on
the oracle's draw button — has never been explained until this sentence. It
is not a symbol for anything. It was chosen because it is the same mark used
in this course's own diagrams, and repeating one plain, undecorated shape
across a whole term is exactly the kind of pattern this course spends twelve
weeks teaching you to notice in things that aren't marks on a website —
before you know whether it means anything, before anyone tells you why it's
there. [The approach page](/approach/) says more about why it went unnamed
this long.
