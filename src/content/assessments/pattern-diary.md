The first assessment is the smallest and the one every later piece of work
depends on. It asks for a habit, not an argument.

## The brief

> Keep a diary of patterns you notice in your own life for the first four
> weeks of semester, writing down what happened before you write down what you
> think it means.

An entry can be as small as a repeated number on a receipt or as large as a
dream that seemed to predict the next day. What matters is not how dramatic
the pattern is, but whether the entry is honest about the order operations
happened in: notice first, interpret second. A diary full of confident
readings and no raw noticing is not doing what this assessment asks.

## What you submit

The diary itself (a PDF export, a shared notes-app document, or scanned
notebook pages — any format a marker can read start to finish), with entries
dated and legible. No word count is set; entries can be a sentence or a
paragraph, whichever the observation actually needs.

The **consistency** criterion rewards entries spread across the weeks rather
than backfilled the night before the deadline. **Specificity** rewards
concrete detail (what exactly happened, when, where) over vague summary.
**Restraint** rewards noticing without immediately explaining — save the
explaining for the Meaning Autopsy.
