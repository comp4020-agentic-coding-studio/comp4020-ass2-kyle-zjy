Every earlier assessment practises one part of the method on borrowed or
constructed material. This one asks you to run the whole method, honestly, on
the moment from your own life that came closest to feeling like fate.

## The brief

> Write a full account of one real moment that felt meaningfully more than
> coincidental — what happened, what you believed about it at the time, and
> what you can now say about why, using this semester's frameworks.

The two halves are not optional extras — they are the whole assignment. The
first half has to be honest about how the moment actually felt, without
already being explained away; a first half that starts with "of course, this
is just apophenia" has skipped past the thing the essay is supposed to be
about. The second half has to go further than naming a bias — it should show,
with the specific texture of your own case, exactly how the noticing, the
linking and the remembering happened, and what (if anything) would have made
the moment worth trusting rather than merely worth noticing.

## What you submit

A written piece of 2,000–3,000 words. Workshop 6 is built specifically to help
you choose a strong moment and plan the split between the two halves — a
choice made in that session tends to produce a far stronger piece than one
made the week of the deadline.
