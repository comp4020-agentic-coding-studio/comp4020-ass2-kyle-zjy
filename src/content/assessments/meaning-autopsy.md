Where the Pattern Diary asks you to notice, the Meaning Autopsy asks you to
properly take one case apart — in public, on paper, with the same honesty the
diary demanded of the noticing.

## The brief

> Choose one entry from your Pattern Diary and give it a full autopsy: what
> happened, why it felt meaningful, and what the strongest available
> explanation actually is.

Pick the entry you were most tempted to call "too weird to be a coincidence."
A strong autopsy does three things in order: describes the event without
already explaining it away, names what made it feel significant at the time,
and then applies at least two of the course's frameworks from weeks 1 through
7 — apophenia, base rates, omen-reading, dream theory, synchronicity, the
Barnum effect, or projective symbol systems, whichever genuinely fit the case.
The frameworks should explain the case, not just be name-dropped near it.

## What you submit

A written piece of 1,200–1,800 words, in whatever format your marker can open
and comment on (a shared document is easiest). Workshops 2 and 3 exist
specifically to help you choose a strong case and pressure-test the draft
before submission — a case chosen and drafted the night before rarely survives
either checklist.
