Build an Oracle asks you to design and build a small working system that
gives readings, the same shape as the systems studied in weeks 6 through 9 —
and to be completely transparent about its mechanism in a way none of those
traditions historically were. This clinic is where the design gets decided,
before the building starts.

## Before the workshop

Have a rough idea of what your oracle asks the user and what kind of thing it
gives back. It doesn't need to be original — several of the strongest past
projects have been honest, disclosed versions of an existing tradition (a
transparent tarot, a disclosed horoscope).

## In the workshop

Sanjay runs a build clinic format: pitch your oracle's concept and mechanism
in two minutes, get questions from the group, particularly on the one point
that trips up most first drafts — where does the "reading" actually come
from, mechanically, and have you said so plainly.

## Afterwards

Start building. The mechanism disclosure isn't a footnote you add at the end —
it's graded as part of the piece, so it's worth drafting alongside the build
rather than after it.
